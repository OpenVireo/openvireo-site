The short version

Pick 1Password if you need Android or Safari, want to share single items with ongoing access, or want a long-established company with published security assessments.

Pick OpenVireo if you want your passwords in an open format you can take anywhere, a free plan, desktop features such as auto-type into any app, or the option to run the server yourself.

Where OpenVireo is different

Your data stays in an open format

OpenVireo stores every vault as a KeePass (KDBX) file. Dozens of apps can open it, so you're never locked in. 1Password uses its own format and exports to 1PUX or CSV.

An account is optional

Use OpenVireo with local files and nothing else, or add a free account later for sync and sharing. 1Password always needs an account.

Run the server yourself

The OpenVireo Sync Server is open source and free, with no plans or limits. It syncs your own devices; sharing between people needs a hosted account. 1Password is hosted only.

Made for desktop power users

Auto-type logins into any app on Windows, macOS and Linux, unlock with a YubiKey or a key file, and use the built-in SSH agent. 1Password's Universal Autofill fills desktop apps on macOS only.

A free plan with everything that matters

OpenVireo Free syncs a vault on all your devices, with two-step verification, account recovery, share links and a service account. 1Password has a 14-day trial and no free plan.

Less for a family

OpenVireo Families is $50 a year for 6 people. 1Password Families is $5.99 a month billed yearly ($71.88) for 5 people, after a first-year offer.

Where 1Password is ahead

  • More platforms. 1Password has an Android app and a Safari extension. OpenVireo has neither yet.
  • Finer sharing. You can share single items with ongoing access, invite guests to a vault, and hide vaults with Travel Mode. OpenVireo shares whole vaults, and single items only through share links.
  • Published security assessments. 1Password publishes third-party assessments and yearly penetration test reports, and holds ISO 27001 and SOC 2 certifications. OpenVireo hasn't had a third-party audit yet; one is planned.
  • Security keys for sign-in. 1Password accepts hardware security keys as a second factor. OpenVireo accounts use an authenticator app.
  • Track record. 1Password has been around for nearly two decades. OpenVireo is new, even if its desktop app builds on KeePassXC's long history.

How the security compares

OpenVireo's account design follows 1Password's in one important way: your keys come from your password and a random 128-bit Secret Key that's created on your device and never sent to the server. If anyone stole the server's data, they couldn't guess their way into your vault from your password alone. OpenVireo signs you in with OPAQUE, so your password never reaches the server, not even during sign-in.

The biggest structural difference: 1Password encrypts each item separately, while OpenVireo encrypts each vault as one KeePass file. That's what keeps vaults readable by any KeePass app, and it's also why OpenVireo shares whole vaults rather than single items.

On openness: OpenVireo's desktop app, browser extension, sync server and SDK are open source, so anyone can read the code that encrypts your data on your computer. The web app, iPhone app and account service are not open source. 1Password is closed source but publishes third-party assessments. OpenVireo doesn't have one yet; an independent audit is planned.

Switching from 1Password

  1. In the 1Password desktop app, export your data as a 1PUX file.
  2. Install OpenVireo, choose Database › Import… and pick 1Password Export in the Import Wizard. See Importing from 1Password.
  3. Securely delete the export file. It isn't encrypted.
  4. To use your passwords on your iPhone and in the browser, move the database to a free account.
