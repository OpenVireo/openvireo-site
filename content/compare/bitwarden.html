The short version

Pick Bitwarden if you need Android, want emergency access, or want a service with years of published audits.

Pick OpenVireo if you want your passwords in an open KeePass file, an app that works with no account, a Secret Key on top of your password, desktop features such as auto-type into any app, or a lower price on every paid plan.

Where OpenVireo is different

A Secret Key as well as your password

OpenVireo mixes a random 128-bit Secret Key, made on your device, into your account keys. If the server's data were ever stolen, guessing your password wouldn't be enough. Bitwarden's keys come from your master password alone.

Your data stays in an open format

Every OpenVireo vault is a KeePass (KDBX) file that dozens of apps can open, and you can use it without an account at all. Bitwarden needs an account and exports to JSON or CSV.

Made for desktop power users

Auto-type logins into any app on Windows, macOS and Linux, and unlock with a YubiKey or a key file. Bitwarden fills logins in browsers and on mobile.

One-time codes on the free plan

OpenVireo generates TOTP codes in every app, on every plan. Bitwarden stores the keys for free but needs a paid plan to generate codes.

Lower prices

Every paid plan costs less than Bitwarden's: Premium is $18 a year against $19.80, Families $40 against $47.88, Teams $40 per member a year against $48, and Business $60 against Enterprise at $72.

Secrets for your servers included

Every OpenVireo organization gets service accounts for the CLI, the GitHub Action and Kubernetes, one on Free. Bitwarden sells this as a separate product, Secrets Manager.

Where Bitwarden is ahead

  • More platforms. Bitwarden has an Android app. OpenVireo doesn't have one yet.
  • Published audits, every year. Bitwarden publishes reports from firms such as Cure53 every year. OpenVireo hasn't had a third-party audit yet; one is planned.
  • Emergency access. Bitwarden lets a trusted contact reach your vault if something happens to you. OpenVireo doesn't have this.
  • Security keys for sign-in, free. Bitwarden accepts FIDO2 security keys on every plan. OpenVireo accounts use an authenticator app.
  • More of its code is public. The code for all of Bitwarden's apps is on GitHub, with GPLv3 builds published there, though the store and download builds are now commercially licensed. OpenVireo's web app, iPhone app and account service are not open source.
  • Finer sharing. Bitwarden shares collections of items inside an organization. OpenVireo shares whole vaults, and single items only through share links.

How the security compares

Both encrypt your data on your device, so the server only stores ciphertext. The difference is what protects that ciphertext if the server's data ever leaked. With Bitwarden, it's the strength of your master password. With OpenVireo, it's your password combined with a 128-bit Secret Key that never leaves your devices. The same design is used by 1Password. OpenVireo also signs you in with OPAQUE, so your password never reaches the server, not even during sign-in.

Bitwarden encrypts each item separately, which allows sharing at the item and collection level. OpenVireo encrypts each vault as one KeePass file, which keeps it readable by any KeePass app.

Bitwarden backs its design with a public record of third-party audits. OpenVireo doesn't have one yet. An independent audit is planned; until then, you'll need to judge it on its open-source code and design.

Switching from Bitwarden

  1. In Bitwarden, export your vault in the .json format (not the encrypted one).
  2. Install OpenVireo, choose Database › Import… and pick Bitwarden in the Import Wizard. See Importing from Bitwarden.
  3. Securely delete the export file. It isn't encrypted.
  4. To use your passwords on your iPhone and in the browser, move the database to a free account.
