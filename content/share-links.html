A share link gives a copy of an entry, or some text, to someone who doesn't use OpenVireo. They open it in any browser, with no account, no app and no sign-in. You make links in the web app, OpenVireo for desktop and OpenVireo for iOS, from an entry of a vault in your account.

Making a link

  • Web app: select an entry and click the link button above it.
  • Desktop: right-click an entry and choose Share Link….
  • iOS: open an entry and tap the link button.
  • Command line: ov share ov://Vault/Entry.

Choose which fields to include, when the link expires (1 hour to 30 days) and how often it can be opened (any number of times, once, or 5 times). You can also require a passcode, which you send separately. To share text that isn't in a vault, use Share text under Shared links.

What the person who opens it sees

A page that says something was shared with them, with a Reveal button. Nothing is shown until they press it, so a chat app or mail scanner that previews the link doesn't use up a one-time link. After that they see the fields you chose, each with a copy button, and the current code if you shared a one-time code.

Why the link has a # in it

The part after # is the key that decrypts what you shared. Browsers never send that part to any server, so it stays between you and the person you send the link to. Your device encrypts the copy with that key before it uploads anything, and our servers keep only the encrypted copy. We can't read what you shared.

Anyone who has the whole link can open it until it expires or reaches its limit, so send it the way you'd send the secret itself. A passcode adds a second secret: the link alone, or the passcode alone, opens nothing, and five wrong passcodes end the link.

A link holds a copy. Changing or deleting the entry later doesn't change what the link shows. Sharing a one-time code shares its secret, so the person can make codes for that account even after the link ends.

Seeing and revoking your links

Shared links (on the web: Account; desktop: Account → Shared Links…; iOS: Settings) lists your links, how often each was opened, and when. Revoking a link stops it at once and deletes the encrypted copy. A link also ends when it expires, when it has been opened as often as you allowed, when you lose access to its vault, or when your organization turns share links off.

In organizations

Owners and admins choose who can make links from the organization's vaults: everyone, only owners and admins, or nobody. Links from organization vaults are in its audit log, without the address of whoever opened them.

Plans

Every plan can make share links. On the free plan you can have 5 open links at a time, each lasting up to 7 days; Premium and every organization plan have no limit on links, up to 30 days each. See pricing.
